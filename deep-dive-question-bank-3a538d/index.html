Stage 2 · Résumé Deep Dive

Deep-Dive Question Bank

The running order I use for a résumé deep dive — plus a build-your-own template for spinning up a brand-new role from a blank page.

Draft Working outline. QA scenarios populated from real material. Companion to the interview playbooks. Two uses in one doc: the Roles block is a ready-to-run universal bank for any role; the Scenarios section carries a template plus a QA worked example.

How I use this in the room

  • The Roles questions are the guardrails: I walk roles oldest → newest and steer to these as the conversation opens up.
  • I won't hit every question on every job — but across all jobs I aim to cover each at least once.
  • Ask "How?" relentlessly; interrupt gracefully; stop and drill at any inconsistency.
1

Scratchpad

Opener / warm-up.

  • Once onboarded and understanding the context here, what are the first places you'll want to explore from a [role] perspective?
  • (Show our process flow — e.g., the Miro board.)
2

Roles

The per-job bank — ask across each job, oldest → newest. Ready to use as-is for any role; this is the starting point when you have nothing.

  • Why did you choose to join the company?
  • What did you inherit coming into this role?
  • What was the organizational context? Who were your peers, your manager? Were you part of a big team, small team?
  • What were your quantifiable accomplishments in this role?
  • How did you achieve those results?
  • What was your biggest failure in the role?
  • What challenges did you face and how did you overcome them?
  • What was your manager's style and how did you work together?
  • What did you enjoy most and least about the role?
  • If we get to the reference stage, what should I expect to hear when I speak with your manager?
  • Why did you choose to move on from this role?

Probes to layer under the above — my style, steer to these as needed

  • (Charter) What were you hired to do — how was success measured in this role?
  • How much were you the driver of the results vs. the team?
  • How were you measured?
  • Three P's: vs. last year? vs. the plan? vs. your peers?
  • (Light technical depth) Not just "did you do X" — "did you understand X?"
3

Scenarios

Role-specific — the only part you rebuild per role. The Roles block above is universal; this is where the role's real depth goes.

Build-your-own template · brand-new role

Draft 1–2 scenarios plus a few questions in each bucket:

  • Core convictions — "What strong convictions do you hold about [domain]?" Reveals depth and taste.
  • Scenario / judgment — a real situation from our world: "here's how our process works today; where does [role] fit and what value would that bring?"
  • Technical judgment — "when is X worth doing / how do you know it's working?" Tests understanding, not trivia.
  • Working style & beliefs — process improvements that worked / didn't; an impossible-deadline story; how they'd approach a concrete piece of work.
  • Passion signal — "if you gave a conference talk, what would it be about?"

QA worked example · a filled-in instance

Convictions What strong convictions do you have about quality and QA testing? · How do you gauge your contribution — what makes you feel valuable? · Describe your ideal QA role.

Scenario 1 — SDLC fit Here's our process flow and where QA sits today (walk the Miro board) — where do you see QA fitting, and what value would that bring? · How would QA fit an anytime-deployment paradigm? · What about testing in production? · Closing loops: how do you find where bugs come from and feed that back?

Scenario 2 — automation & test judgment When is it worth writing an automated test, and how do you know you're getting value? · What do you do about a flaky/intermittent test — and what are the dangers of flaky automation? · We have 78 e2e smoke tests that take 12 minutes to run — thoughts? · Given a ticket with acceptance criteria, how would you test it, and what do you want to know before you start?

Working style What workflow/process improvements have you had success with — and which haven't worked? · Ever had a project with an impossible deadline?

Passion If you gave a conference talk, what would it be about?

4

Closing Questions

  • What are you looking for — the environment you want, how you want to be empowered, what decisions you want to make?
  • Where are you at in your job search? Do you have other offers on the table? If you had multiple offers in front of you, what 3 criteria are you using to decide which one you take?
