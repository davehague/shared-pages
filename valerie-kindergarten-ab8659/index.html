Readiness, the non-academic kind

⏳

Practice waiting

The principal's #1 Kindergarten-readiness skill. Take turns as a family — brushing teeth, getting in the car, picking the movie. Modeling patience builds the self-control school asks for all day.

🧺

Give one weekly job

A small, real responsibility builds confidence. For Valerie's age: get the mail, set the table, pick up toys when done, or get her own snack.

How the first days work

⏰

Daily rhythm

8:00 AM — doors open (no supervision before then, so never drop early)
8:10 AM — classes begin / bell rings
2:45–2:50 PM — Kindergarten pickup

🚗

Car drop-off

Use door #15 (Hennessey side) or door #2 (Whitney side). Kids exit passenger side only — that may mean moving her car seat, so practice it early. Parents stay in the car, follow the path, don't park in drop-off lanes or staff lots.

🎒

Pickup: the "Kindergarten pad"

Kindergartners are walked to daycare vans, buses, or the Kindergarten pad out front — an adult picks her up directly, between 2:45 and 2:50 PM. Park along Whitney or Hennessey; never on Rieber at arrival or dismissal (buses, heavy traffic).

🔄

First-week teacher cycling

Kindergartners rotate through every K teacher the first week so staff can build balanced classes. You'll hear from the whole K team that week, then get assigned to one teacher after the first full week.

🦉

Valerie's Kindergarten team

Ms. Niskanen (principal), Mrs. Packert (counselor), Mrs. Thompson (secretary), and K teachers Ms. Hoffman, Ms. Martin, Mrs. Ralston & Mrs. J. Shaffer — plus Ms. Gupta, Mrs. Lebold & Mr. Meyer in the Specialized Learning Center and intervention specialists Mrs. Lewis, Mrs. J. Kress & Mrs. Baker.

🤗

"We're a guilt-free zone"

Ms. Niskanen's words: miss an event and it's genuinely fine. Recap emails follow every family event, and there's a lot of email this season. Skim, don't stress.
